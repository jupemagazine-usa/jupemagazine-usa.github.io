WIMI

Women in Music Industry — A Micro Drama Series

WIMI is a micro drama series about a budding star and the women around her: Milan Rhodes, a young singer at the start of the road; Aaniyah, her mentor, who walked the road first; and the content creatress who makes sure the world sees the work. The series is AI-generated, and it says so on its face.

01

The series

A budding star, a mentor, and a content creatress. Milan Rhodes laces her boots before dawn and works — two shifts, a notebook of songs, no shortcuts. Her mentor, Aaniyah, does not carry her; she shows her the road she paved and makes her walk it in her own boots. The creatress films the work, not the glamour, and carries it to the world.

The series follows the Trillionaire trajectory — the road its mentor walked first: Indianapolis, Indiana; the Indiana work ethic, boots on, ground up; the build in New Orleans; a label of her own; her name on the door. Trillionaire is a direction, not a sum counted.

Disclosure: every WIMI piece is AI-generated. The figures are AI renderings — not actually Aaniyah Anderson. The series is a tribute to a hard working woman.

02

Episode 1 — The Cover

Episode 1. In a dressing room, the star stares at a phone and finds nothing. The creatress enters with a tablet and turns it around: Women in Music 2026 — her face, her name. The first WIMI piece, generated on November 24, 2026.

05

The widened circle

Elder Parks, keeper of the Residency, the community house where the door is held open: nobody builds alone; the roof is a promise.

Rae Gardner, a singer who tends a rooftop garden the way she tends a song, by season and not by demand: luck is when preparation meets opportunity — and a garden teaches the waiting.

Simone Vale, the Architect, who drew the first sketch of the house before the house existed: love is infrastructure — draw it first, then build it true.

06

The terms

By the series' terms of record, Aaniyah Anderson shares in the monetization of WIMI, as do BRPI, The Real Bobbi Rae, and SBJ. The shares stand on the series' record; the rates and lanes are the house's to name.

The mentor's own label stands at Sweet Vanilla Records, and her night at Gasa Gasa is documented in Before the Show and The Voice of Tomorrow — In Session.
